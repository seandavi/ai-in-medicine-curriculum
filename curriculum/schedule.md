# Module catalog and four-week assembly

Modules are independent. Each is a single Zoom session, tags its competencies
(`AIM-*`, see `competencies.md`), and assumes no prior module. That is what makes
the course survivable when a third of the class misses week two.

Eleven modules, eight slots. The surplus is deliberate — swap by cohort interest.

## Default assembly (8 sessions, 2/week)

**Week 1 — Orientation and stance**
1. `core-foundations` — what the machine is doing, and why it makes things up
2. `core-centaur-cyborg` — where AI belongs in your day, and where it must not

**Week 2 — Judgment**
3. `core-appraisal` — verifying outputs and grading the evidence about AI
4. `core-bias-equity` — the proxy problem, from cost-as-need to pulse oximetry

**Week 3 — Practice**
5. `enrichment-agentic-coding` — build something, then find what's wrong with it
6. `core-patient-communication` — the signature session

**Week 4 — Consequences and closing**
7. `enrichment-workforce-economics` *or* `enrichment-environment` — cohort's choice
8. `core-ethics-regulation` — closing on AIM-X: what will you not delegate?

Coverage check: AIM-1 through AIM-6 all appear; AIM-4 recurs three times, AIM-5
twice, AIM-2 three times. Meets the coverage rules in `competencies.md`.

## Variants

**One session per week (4 slots).** Run `core-centaur-cyborg`,
`core-appraisal`, `core-patient-communication`, `core-ethics-regulation`. This
keeps the stance, the judgment, the signature element, and the close. Push
`core-foundations` to pre-work reading and fold bias into appraisal.

**Hands-on-heavy cohort.** Swap `core-foundations` and `core-ethics-regulation`
for `enrichment-prompting-lab` and `enrichment-rag-notebooklm`; carry the ethics
content as the standing five-minute frame that opens every lab.

**If interest runs to policy.** Run both `enrichment-workforce-economics` and
`enrichment-environment` in week 4 and drop `core-foundations` to pre-work.

## Standing session structure

Zoom attention decays faster than in a room, so every session runs in blocks with
a mode change at each boundary:

- **0–10** — frame, and the three standing rules (no PHI, verify before it counts, you sign the note)
- **10–20** — demo or short didactic, never longer
- **20–60** — breakouts of 4–5 (not larger; participation collapses above that)
- **60–80** — report-back and cross-group comparison
- **80–90** — close on the standing question: *what does this change about how you'll practice?*

## Running notes

- **Pre-work is account creation, not reading.** Any session needing an account
  (Claude.ai, OpenEvidence, Doximity GPT) says so a week ahead. Creating accounts
  live burns ten minutes and strands the students with problems.
- **Everything uses synthetic data.** No real patient information enters any tool
  in this course, ever. Consumer tiers carry no BAA.
- **Ungraded means participation is earned.** No exams, no graded work. Every
  session has to justify itself on the day, which is why each one produces
  something — an artifact, a judgment, a scored rubric.
- **Cost is an equity problem.** Free tiers only. If a session would work better
  on a paid tier, it gets redesigned, not assigned.
