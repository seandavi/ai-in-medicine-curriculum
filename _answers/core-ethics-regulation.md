## Part B — model answers for Mr. Alvarez

1. **Box.** B (AI departed from standard; physician followed), by way of the
   signed plan: "continue apixaban" on a false premise. A defensible
   alternative: "not on the grid; the scribe made no recommendation, and the
   liability is ordinary documentation negligence." Either earns credit if the
   recommendation is named.
2. **Elements.** Duty (clear). Breach: signing a note that contradicts what the
   patient said; the defense is that review of a drafted note at that hour
   with that load is within the standard, which is exactly what is untested.
   Causation is hardest: the stroke followed a two-week gap the patient
   created; the plaintiff must show the erroneous note changed what happened
   next (e.g., no one addressed the nosebleeds or the stopped drug at the
   visit or after).
3. **Deciding facts** (any one, with direction): an auto-inserted attestation
   the intern did not read (toward breach, and toward the vendor/institution);
   a written institutional policy requiring plan-versus-conversation review
   (toward breach if ignored, toward defense if followed); Mr. Alvarez's
   knowledge of the scribe (consent claim, separate from malpractice); the
   time of day and note count (mitigates breach in a jury's eyes, per the
   Tobia and Tacconelli findings on reasonableness); whether the intern
   addressed the stopped apixaban verbally even though the note was wrong
   (breaks causation).
4. **Sepsis alert.** Fails criterion 1 (analyzes monitor signals) and criterion
   4 (time-critical). It is a device. Whether that matters to the intern:
   clearance tells her the manufacturer satisfied FDA, not that the alert was
   validated on her unit's population (Wu 2021; Chouffani El Fassi 2024).

## Standing question — what to look for

Not graded. A passing answer names one part of judgment and one condition
that could prove them wrong. A slogan ("I will always be the final decision-maker") gets sent
back with: "what would have to be true for you to stop being?" Read six
aloud in the live session and do not adjudicate.

## Instructor prep

Open the FDA CDS page and the HTI-5 fact sheet the morning of the session.
If either has moved, say so in the first five minutes; it is the best possible
opening for the scavenger hunt.
